  • Running out of cash no longer freezes the game. With the finance desk and the option to abandon a program, simulated companies hit a true dead end in under 0.3% of games.
  • Outcomes are skewed like the real industry: the median company loses money while the mean is pulled up by rare blockbusters.
  • Each lever has a trade‑off. Debt keeps ownership but adds insolvency risk. Partnering and out‑licensing raise the chance of ending above your starting stake but cap the upside.
  • Rare disease is the strongest path, consistent with its higher success rate and cheaper trials. It is the main knob to turn if you want more even areas.
  • Capitalised cost per approval runs high because the game uses 2011–20 success rates (7.9% from Phase I), lower than the 11.8% in the sample behind DiMasi's $2.6B.
  • Area, modality, target and biomarker effects are multiplied together, which assumes they are independent. Market sizes, price levels and the response to each commercial lever are teaching assumptions tuned to match the peak‑sales benchmarks, not measured values.
